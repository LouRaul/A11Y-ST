Templates do two things that matter enormously when you're building a knowledge wiki over months and years: they reduce the friction of starting a new entry, and they ensure that entries written today are still consistent and readable long after you've forgotten the context around them. Instead of facing a blank page every time you sit down to capture a book you've read or an idea worth exploring, you start with a ready-made structure and fill in only what's relevant. The result is a wiki that stays coherent as it grows, without requiring you to manually impose structure after the fact.

## Quick Note Template

Use this template when you need to capture something fast — a fleeting thought, a useful link, or a quick observation — without committing to a full entry right away. Quick notes are meant to be small; if one grows into something larger, migrate it into a more structured template.

```md
## [Date] — [Title]
**Tags:** #topic #keyword
**Summary:** One or two sentences.
**Links:** Related pages or resources.
```

## Idea Entry Template

Ideas rarely arrive fully formed. This template gives each idea a home at any stage of development — from a vague hunch to a fully fleshed-out concept. The **Status** field (Seed / Sprouting / Blooming) lets you see at a glance which ideas are still germinating and which are ready to act on.

```md
## [Idea Title]
**Status:** Seed / Sprouting / Blooming
**Context:** Why this idea matters.
**Potential:** What could this become?
**Next Steps:** What to explore next.
**Links:** Related notes or resources.
```

## Book Entry Template

A book entry lets you turn a passive reading experience into a durable record of what you learned and what resonated. Fill in the **Key Takeaways** while the book is fresh, and return to the **Memorable Quote** field whenever a line stops you mid-chapter.

```md
## [Book Title] — [Author]
**Genre:** ...
**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Status:** To Read / Reading / Finished
**Summary:** 2-3 sentences.
**Key Takeaways:**
- Takeaway 1
- Takeaway 2
**Memorable Quote:** "..."
```

## Article Entry Template

Use this template whenever you save an article worth revisiting — a long read, a research summary, an opinion piece that shifted your thinking. The **Why it matters** field is the most important one: a single sentence that captures why you saved it in the first place, written in your own words.

```md
## [Article Title]
**Source:** [Publication]
**URL:** https://...
**Date Saved:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Tags:** #topic
**Why it matters:** One sentence.
**Key Points:**
- Point 1
- Point 2
```

## Topic Note Template

Topic notes are the evergreen backbone of your wiki — structured, linkable entries on subjects you want to understand deeply over time. Unlike quick notes or journal entries, topic notes are meant to be revised and expanded as your understanding grows. Use clear section headings so future-you can navigate the note quickly.

```md
## [Topic Title]
**Category:** ...
**Last Updated:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Summary:** 2-3 sentences describing the topic.

### Key Concepts
- Concept 1: brief explanation
- Concept 2: brief explanation

### Why It Matters
Why is this topic relevant to your work or learning?

### Open Questions
- Question you haven't answered yet.

### Resources
- [Resource Title](https://...)
```

## Journal Entry Template

A daily journal entry doesn't need to be long to be valuable. This template keeps each entry consistent and reflective without demanding too much time. The **Tomorrow** field is particularly useful — ending each entry with one clear intention makes the next day easier to start.

```md
## [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Mood/Energy:** 🌤️
**What happened:** ...
**What I learned:** ...
**Gratitude:** ...
**Tomorrow:** ...
```

<Tip>
  Treat these templates as starting points, not rigid rules. If a field never feels useful to you, remove it. If you always find yourself adding the same custom field, make it part of your personal version of the template. The best template is the one you'll actually use every time.
</Tip>
