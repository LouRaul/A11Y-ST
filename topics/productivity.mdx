Productivity knowledge is only useful if you can actually retrieve and apply it when you need it most. This section of your wiki is where you document the systems, methods, and techniques you've tested — not as a wishlist, but as a living record of what has worked for you, what hasn't, and what you've adapted over time. By capturing these insights here rather than leaving them buried in half-finished books or forgotten podcasts, you build a personal operating manual that keeps improving as you do.

## Key Areas

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Time Management">
    Time management techniques help you allocate your hours intentionally and protect the blocks that matter most. Notes in this area might cover time-blocking strategies, the Pomodoro technique, calendar audits, or frameworks like Eisenhower's matrix for separating urgent tasks from important ones. Document not just how a technique works, but how you've adapted it to fit your actual schedule.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Task Systems">
    Task systems give your to-do list structure and meaning. This includes approaches like Getting Things Done (GTD), the PARA method, Tiago Forte's Second Brain, and simpler capture-and-review loops. Record the core principles of each system you've explored, note what resonated, and track any hybrid approach you've settled on.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Focus & Deep Work">
    Focus strategies help you do your best thinking without interruption. Draw on concepts from Cal Newport's deep work framework, ideas around single-tasking, environment design, and digital minimalism. Note which conditions — time of day, physical setup, notification settings — consistently support your most productive sessions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Habits & Routines">
    Habits and routines are the infrastructure of consistent output. Entries here might cover habit stacking, morning and evening routines, the role of keystone habits, or lessons from books like *Atomic Habits*. Track what you've tried to build, what stuck, and what fell apart — honest reflection is more valuable than polished success stories.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Productivity Entry Format

Use this template when logging a new system, technique, or productivity experiment:

```md
## [System or Technique]
**Category:** Time / Tasks / Focus / Habits
**Source:** Book, article, or personal experiment.
**How It Works:** Brief description.
**My Experience:** What worked, what didn't.
**Verdict:** Still using / Retired / Modified
```

<Tip>
  When you run a productivity experiment — trying a new routine, testing a different task system, or blocking time in a new way — log the outcome in your **Journal** page. Over time, that record becomes some of the most useful data you have about how you actually work best.
</Tip>
