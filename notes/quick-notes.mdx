Quick notes are your first line of defence against forgetting. When a thought, observation, or fragment surfaces — whether you're in the middle of a conversation, reading an article, or drifting off to sleep — the goal is to capture it immediately, without polish or hesitation. This page is your designated space for exactly that: raw, unfiltered captures that you can refine later.

## How to Use Quick Notes

Follow these three steps to make quick notes work for you rather than pile up into noise.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Jot it down immediately — don't edit">
    Write the thought exactly as it comes to you. Resist the urge to rephrase, expand, or tidy it up. Speed and fidelity matter more than presentation at this stage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag with a topic or keyword">
    Add one or two tags that describe the subject area. Tags make it easy to cluster related notes when you sit down for your weekly review. Keep tags short and consistent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review weekly and promote or discard">
    Set aside time each week to skim your quick notes. Promote anything worth developing into a full entry or resource. Archive or delete anything that no longer resonates — not every note needs to survive.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Sample Quick Note

Here is what a typical quick note looks like in practice. Notice it is brief, tagged, and makes no attempt to be comprehensive.

<Note>
  **2024-06-12 — Spaced repetition for code snippets**

  **Tags:** #learning #dev-tools

  **Summary:** Could use Anki-style flashcard logic to resurface useful but rarely used code patterns — similar to how vocabulary decks work. Worth prototyping.
</Note>

## Quick Note Format

Use the template below as a lightweight structure for every note you capture. Copy it, fill in the blanks, and move on — the whole thing should take under two minutes.

```md
## [Date] — [Title]
**Tags:** #topic #keyword
**Summary:** One or two sentences.
```

<Tip>
  Keep each note to three lines or fewer. If you find yourself writing more, stop — you have probably crossed into full-entry territory. Link your note to an existing topic page using a `[[ ]]` reference so it stays connected to the rest of your wiki.
</Tip>
