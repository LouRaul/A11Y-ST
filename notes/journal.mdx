A journal sits at the heart of any serious knowledge practice. While other sections of your wiki capture facts, ideas, and resources, your journal captures the texture of your thinking — how you felt about a decision, what surprised you today, and what you want to carry forward. Over time, a consistent journaling habit gives you a firsthand record of how your perspective shifts, deepens, and occasionally reverses, which is something no external source can provide.

## Journal Entry Format

Use the template below to structure each daily entry. You do not need to fill every field completely — even a few lines per prompt adds up to a meaningful record over weeks and months.

```md
## [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Mood/Energy:** 🌤️
**What happened:** ...
**What I learned:** ...
**Gratitude:** ...
**Tomorrow:** ...
```

## Review Cadence

Writing entries is only half the practice. Regular review is what transforms isolated observations into genuine insight. Work through each level of review on schedule.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Daily — write a short entry">
    Spend five to ten minutes at the end of each day completing an entry. Keep it honest and brief. You are writing for your future self, not an audience — clarity matters more than eloquence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Weekly — review the week's entries">
    On a chosen day each week, read back through every entry from the past seven days. Note any recurring themes, unresolved tensions, or ideas that kept resurfacing. Add a short weekly summary entry if something significant stands out.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monthly — identify patterns and themes">
    At the end of each month, scan your weekly summaries and look for broader patterns. Are there habits that are helping or hindering you? Are there topics you keep returning to? Use this review to adjust your focus for the month ahead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Yearly — reflect on growth and set intentions">
    Once a year, read back through your monthly summaries. Notice how your thinking, priorities, and energy have evolved. Celebrate genuine progress and set clear intentions — not just goals — for the year ahead.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  If you prefer to keep journal entries private, consider archiving them in a separate, offline location rather than publishing them alongside the rest of your wiki. You can maintain a public-facing "learnings" summary while keeping the raw, personal entries in a local folder or encrypted notebook.
</Note>
