The best ideas rarely arrive fully formed. Most start as a vague hunch, an unexpected connection, or a question you cannot stop thinking about. Capturing them at that rough, early stage is what separates a thriving knowledge practice from one that only records what is already finished. This page gives every half-formed thought a proper home, complete with enough context to pick it back up days or weeks later.

## Idea Entry Format

Use the template below for every idea you log. Fill in only what you know right now — it is fine to leave fields sparse when you are just starting out.

```md
## [Idea Title]
**Status:** Seed / Sprouting / Blooming
**Context:** Why this idea matters.
**Potential:** What could this become?
**Next Steps:** What to explore next.
```

## Idea Statuses

Every idea moves through a lifecycle. Use the statuses below to signal where each one sits — and to help you decide what deserves your attention during your next review session.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="🌱 Seed — Just captured, no further development">
    A Seed idea has been written down but nothing more. You have not researched it, tested it, or discussed it with anyone. The entry might be a single sentence. That is perfectly fine — the point is that the idea exists somewhere outside your head and will not be lost.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="🌿 Sprouting — Actively exploring, some research done">
    A Sprouting idea is one you have started to investigate. You may have read a few articles, sketched out a rough plan, or talked it through with someone. The Context and Potential fields should be reasonably filled in, and you have at least one concrete Next Step listed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="🌸 Blooming — Ready to become a full project or resource">
    A Blooming idea has enough definition and momentum to graduate into a dedicated project, resource page, or journal entry. Promote it out of this list and give it its own space in your wiki. Archive the original idea entry with a link to where it went.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Schedule a monthly "idea archaeology" session where you scroll back through older entries. Ideas that felt irrelevant three months ago often become surprisingly relevant after you have gained new context — revisiting them regularly keeps your creative inventory alive.
</Tip>
