A curated link library beats browser bookmarks in every way that matters. Instead of an unorganized folder of URLs you'll never find again, you get a searchable, annotated collection where each link has context — why you saved it, what it's for, and how it connects to the rest of your knowledge. This page is the home for every web resource worth keeping, organized so you can actually find it when you need it.

## Link Entry Format

Use this template each time you save a link worth keeping. The "why save it" line is the most important field — skip it and the link becomes just another anonymous URL in six months.

```md
## [Link Title]
**URL:** https://...
**Category:** Website / Newsletter / Community / etc.
**Tags:** #topic
**Why save it:** One sentence.
```

## Categories

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Websites">
    General reference sites, documentation hubs, encyclopedias, and authoritative single-topic resources you return to repeatedly. Prioritize sites with durable, well-maintained content over those that go stale quickly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Newsletters">
    Email newsletters worth subscribing to and archiving. For each newsletter, note the cadence (daily, weekly, monthly) and the specific angle or audience it serves — this helps you decide whether to keep subscribing as your interests evolve.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Communities">
    Forums, Discord servers, subreddits, Slack groups, and other spaces where people discuss topics you care about. Note the signal-to-noise ratio and whether the community is active — a quiet forum with great archives can be just as valuable as a busy one.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Courses & Learning">
    Online courses, tutorial series, video lectures, and structured learning resources. Record whether you've completed them, what you found most useful, and any supplementary materials worth pairing with each one.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  Periodically audit your link collection for dead URLs. Links rot — pages move, sites shut down, and content gets deleted. Set a reminder every six months to scan your entries, archive anything that no longer resolves, and check whether a saved resource has been superseded by something better.
</Note>
