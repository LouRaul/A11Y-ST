Getting the most out of this wiki means knowing where things live and how they connect. The structure is intentionally lightweight — a handful of top-level sections, consistent internal links, and a powerful search bar — so you can move from a vague question to a concrete answer with as few steps as possible. Follow the guide below to orient yourself and start navigating with confidence.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with the Introduction">
    Read the [Introduction](/introduction) page first to understand the overall structure of the wiki, the organizational philosophy, and what each section contains. A few minutes here will save you a lot of searching later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse by Topic">
    Head to the [Topics](/topics/technology) section whenever you want to explore a subject in depth. Entries here are organized by theme and linked to related notes and resources, making it easy to follow a thread as far as you want to take it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explore Resources">
    Visit the [Resources](/resources/books) section to find curated books, articles, tools, and external links. Each entry includes a short annotation so you know what you're getting before you click through.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the Reference Section">
    Turn to the [Reference](/reference/glossary) section for quick lookups. It holds glossary definitions, reusable templates, and any material you find yourself returning to on a regular basis — think of it as the wiki's index.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Search Tips

The search bar is the fastest way to find anything in the wiki, especially once the number of entries grows.

<Note>
  Press **Cmd+K** on Mac or **Ctrl+K** on Windows and Linux to open the search bar from anywhere in the wiki — no need to reach for the mouse.
</Note>

- **Search by keyword** — type the main concept you're looking for rather than a full phrase; shorter queries return broader, more useful results.
- **Search by tag** — if you remember a tag associated with an entry, include it in your search to narrow results quickly.
- **Use partial words** — the search index supports partial matching, so typing the first few letters of a term is enough to surface relevant entries.
- **Scan the sidebar** — for broad browsing, the sidebar is often faster than search; use it to get a sense of what exists within a section before drilling down.

## Adding New Entries

This wiki is designed to grow over time, and a few simple principles keep it useful as it does. Keep each entry **concise and focused** — one idea, one topic, one resource per page. **Link generously** to related entries using internal links; those connections are what transform a flat list of pages into a genuine knowledge network. Finally, **use tags consistently** to label entries by theme, so cross-section patterns stay visible even as the total number of pages increases. When in doubt, write the entry now and refine it later — a rough note is more valuable than a perfectly planned page that never gets written.
