Saving and annotating articles builds a personal reading archive you can actually use. Rather than bookmarking links that disappear into browser folders, you capture the key points, your reaction, and why the piece mattered — so when a topic comes up weeks later, you have a meaningful record to return to rather than a raw URL you can't place.

## Article Entry Format

Use this template every time you save an article worth keeping. Fill it in while the article is fresh in your mind, not days later.

```md
## [Article Title]
**Source:** [Publication Name]
**URL:** https://...
**Date Saved:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Tags:** #topic #keyword
**Why it matters:** One sentence.
**Key Points:**
- Point 1
- Point 2
```

## How to Curate Well

Not every article you read deserves a permanent entry. Be selective about what earns a place in your archive.

<Note>
  Quality over quantity is the core principle of a useful article collection. A smaller set of deeply annotated entries is far more valuable than hundreds of half-filled stubs. Before saving an article, ask: will I realistically want to find this again, and does it add something I don't already have?
</Note>

- **Save for insight, not interest.** If an article is entertaining but teaches you nothing new, let it go. Reserve entries for pieces that shift your thinking or add a useful reference.
- **Write the "why it matters" line immediately.** This one sentence forces you to articulate the article's value before the clarity of first reading fades.
- **Annotate, don't just copy.** Add your own reaction alongside the key points — agree, disagree, connect it to something else. Your annotations are what separate a personal archive from a generic bookmarks list.
- **Delete or archive stale entries.** If an article's advice is outdated or you've long since internalized its lesson, remove the entry or move it to an archive section so your active collection stays fresh and relevant.

<Tip>
  Use tags consistently and link tagged articles to matching pages in your Topics section. If you save three articles on note-taking systems, a `#note-taking` tag lets you surface all three from a single topic page — turning scattered saves into a coherent resource cluster.
</Tip>
