Keeping a curated book list with personal notes and takeaways transforms reading from a passive hobby into a compounding knowledge practice. Instead of letting insights fade after you close the cover, you capture what mattered, why it mattered, and how it connects to the rest of your thinking — making every book you finish a permanent part of your knowledge base.

## Book Entry Format

Use the template below for every book you add. Consistency makes it easier to scan your library and surface the right book when you need it.

```md
## [Book Title] — [Author]
**Genre:** Non-fiction / Fiction / etc.
**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Status:** To Read / Reading / Finished
**Summary:** 2-3 sentences.
**Key Takeaways:**
- Takeaway 1
- Takeaway 2
**Memorable Quote:** "..."
```

## Reading Strategy

A deliberate reading strategy ensures your book list stays useful rather than becoming a graveyard of good intentions.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose intentionally">
    Read books that serve your current goals. Before adding a book to your list, ask yourself what question it answers or what skill it builds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Take notes while reading">
    Capture highlights, reactions, and page references as you go. Don't rely on memory — write marginalia or keep a running note alongside the book.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a summary entry after finishing">
    Within a day or two of finishing, fill in the book entry template. Recency matters — the synthesis is freshest right after you close the cover.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Revisit key takeaways quarterly">
    Schedule a quarterly review of your finished books. Re-reading your takeaways surfaces patterns and reinforces retention without re-reading the whole book.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Link each book entry to relevant pages in your Topics section. If a book on habit formation connects to a topic on productivity systems, add a cross-reference — this is how isolated notes become a connected knowledge graph.
</Tip>
